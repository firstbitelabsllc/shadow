## Resplit splitter core — how subtotal/tax/tip/extras are divided across participants (iOS)

**Files read:** /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/ReceiptSplitEngine.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/ReceiptSplitCalculator.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/DTOs/SplitResult.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/UseCases/SplitItemUseCase.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/ReceiptItemsFixer.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/Calculators/ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/Calculators/SummaryItemCalculator.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitter/Models/Receipt.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitterTests/Invariants/MathInvariants.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitterTests/TaxTip/TaxTipEdgeCaseInvariantsTests.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitterTests/Invariants/ReceiptSplitEngineInvariantsTests.swift, /Users/leokwan/Development/resplit-ios/ReceiptSplitterTests/Invariants/PropertyBasedEngineInvariantsTests.swift

**Current behavior:** THE CORE MENTAL MODEL: There is NO separate tax/tip apportionment step. The engine computes a per-participant PROPORTION purely from claimed item amounts, then scales the ENTIRE receipt total (which already bundles subtotal + tax + tip + custom extras) by that proportion. Tax and tip ride along inside `totalAmount` and get distributed in the exact same ratio as a person's item consumption — i.e. proportional-by-consumption, never a separate equal-per-head pass.

PIPELINE (driver → engine):
1. `ReceiptSplitCalculator.updateSplits()` (ReceiptSplitCalculator.swift:172-195) builds `ReceiptItemDTO`s from `receipt.items` and crucially passes `totalAmount: receipt.resolvedTotalAmount` (line 187) into `ReceiptSplitEngine.calculateSplit`. The DTO carries only item amount + sharedEqually + participantIds — tax/tip are NOT passed as line items; they are pre-baked into `totalAmount`.
2. `receipt.resolvedTotalAmount` (Receipt.swift:265-280): precedence = user-override total > `ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal` (if > 0) > scanned OCR total. So normally the engine receives the computed total.
3. `ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal` (ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift:5-20) = `itemSum + tax + tip + customExtras`, where tax = `receipt.taxItem?.amount.valueToUse` (line 10), tip = `receipt.tipItem?.amount.valueToUse` (line 11), customExtras = sum of every `summaryItems` whose `type == .custom` (lines 13-18). This is the ONLY place extras enter the total.

ENGINE MATH (ReceiptSplitEngine.calculateSplit, ReceiptSplitEngine.swift:11-95):
- Step 1 (lines 26-41): build `rawAmountsByEach`. `sharedEqually` items split `item.amount / participantIds.count` to EVERY participant (line 28). Individually-claimed items split `item.amount / item.participantIds.count` only to listed claimers (lines 33-39). These raw amounts are SUBTOTAL-domain only (item prices), tax/tip excluded.
- Step 2 (lines 44-51): `proportionsByParticipant[p] = rawAmount[p] / sumOfRawAmounts`. Proportions are derived ONLY from item consumption. Tax/tip have zero influence on the ratio.
- Step 3 (lines 61-64): orphan items (`!sharedEqually && participantIds.isEmpty`) are summed into `orphanAmount` and SUBTRACTED from totalAmount → `distributableAmount = totalAmount - orphanAmount`. Orphans stay on the receipt total but are NOT redistributed (the payer absorbs them); UI copy 'Won't count toward totals' depends on this.
- Step 4 (lines 71-81): `amountOwed[p] = round(proportion[p] * distributableAmount)`. THIS is where tax+tip+custom get folded in: each person's consumption-proportion is applied to the whole distributable pool (subtotal+tax+tip+custom minus orphans), so tax/tip land on each head IN PROPORTION TO their item share. A person who ordered nothing (proportion 0) pays $0 of tax/tip; a person who ordered half the food pays half the tax and half the tip.

SO: SUBTOTAL split = by claimed items (sharedEqually = even across all; individual = even across claimers; orphan = nobody, payer eats it). TAX + TIP + CUSTOM EXTRAS split = proportional to each person's subtotal/item share (NOT equal-per-head), implicitly, because they are bundled into totalAmount and scaled by the consumption-derived proportion.

TIP BASE is a separate, earlier computation: `SummaryItemCalculator.calculatedTip` (SummaryItemCalculator.swift:8-25) = (subtotal or subtotal+tax, per `calculatePostTax`) × tipOption.percent/100. `calculatedSubtotal` (line 4-6) sums EVERY item INCLUDING orphans. So tip-percentage base includes orphans even though orphans are excluded from the engine's distributable pool — an intentional asymmetry pinned by ORPHAN_IN_TIP_BASE_PINNED.

EMPTY-ITEMS CANARY: if items is empty, sumOfRawAmounts = 0, no proportions set, every participant owes $0 regardless of totalAmount (no equal-split fallback). Locked by EMPTY_ITEMS_ALL_ZERO.

**Apportionment:** PROPORTIONAL-BY-CONSUMPTION is the universal rule for everything except the subtotal-item assignment itself. Concretely:
- SUBTOTAL (item prices): item-level. sharedEqually items → equal-per-head across ALL participants; individually-assigned items → equal split across ONLY the listed claimers; orphan items (unassigned, not shared) → assigned to NOBODY, removed from the distributable pool, absorbed by the payer.
- TAX, TIP, and CUSTOM EXTRAS: NOT apportioned by their own rule and NOT equal-per-head. They are summed into `resolvedTotalAmount` (ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift:19) and then the engine multiplies each person's CONSUMPTION proportion (derived solely from item amounts, ReceiptSplitEngine.swift:44-49) by the full distributable pool (ReceiptSplitEngine.swift:73). Net effect: tax+tip+extras are distributed in the SAME ratio as each person's item share — proportional-by-consumption.

WHERE A NEW EXTRA KIND LANDS BY DEFAULT: A new extra (service charge, surcharge, cover, fee, deposit-credit) added naively as a `.custom` SummaryItem would flow into `ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal` via the `customExtras` filter (`summaryItems.filter { $0.type == .custom }`, ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift:13-18), increasing `resolvedTotalAmount`. From there the engine would spread it PROPORTIONAL-BY-CONSUMPTION across participants automatically — same as tax/tip. It would NOT be equal-per-head, would NOT be assignable to a subset, and a NEGATIVE extra (e.g. deposit-credit/discount) would proportionally REDUCE everyone's share but is untested and would risk BOUNDED_LOWER if it drove a proportion*distributable below 0. If instead the new extra were added with any type OTHER than .custom/.tax/.tip (e.g. a brand-new enum case), it would be SILENTLY DROPPED from the total because `calculatedTotal` only sums tax + tip + .custom — it has no catch-all. That is the key trap for a V2 extra-kind: you must either reuse `.custom` or extend `ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal` explicitly.

**Smells / fragility for V2:**
- NO first-class concept of an 'extra' beyond tax/tip — the only catch-all bucket is SummaryItemType.custom. A new extra kind (service charge, cover, fee, deposit-credit) has nowhere typed to live; it must masquerade as .custom or get explicitly added to ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal (ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift:13-19).
- SILENT-DROP TRAP: ReceiptTotalCalculator.calculatedTotal sums ONLY tax + tip + (.custom) summaryItems. Any summary item with a new/other type is silently omitted from resolvedTotalAmount — no catch-all, no assertion. A V2 'fee' type added to the enum without touching this function would vanish from every total and split. (ReceiptTotalCalculator.swift:5-20)
- Tax/tip/extras are NEVER apportionable to a subset or equal-per-head — they are hard-wired to ride the item-consumption proportion. There is no flag, no per-extra split-mode. A 'split the service charge equally even though we split food by item' requirement cannot be expressed today.
- Tip-base asymmetry: tip% base (calculatedSubtotal) INCLUDES orphans, but the engine EXCLUDES orphans from the distributable pool. So on a receipt with orphan items + percentage tip, the tip is computed on a larger base than the pool it's distributed over — intentional but surprising, only pinned by one test (ORPHAN_IN_TIP_BASE_PINNED).
- NO overflow / sanity bound on the total — `calculatedTotal` sums blindly with no `total > N × subtotal` check; explicitly documented as a production gap (TIP_TAX_CUSTOM_OVERFLOW_DETECTION skipped, TaxTipEdgeCaseInvariantsTests.swift:32-37). A garbage extra value would silently inflate everyone's share.
- Negative extras (deposit-credit / discount) are completely untested and would multiply through proportion*distributable; could drive a participant's amountOwed below 0 and trip BOUNDED_LOWER, or produce a negative distributableAmount if extras net negative.
- Currency is hardcoded-fallback 'USD' in two places (ReceiptSplitCalculator.swift:90, Receipt.swift:306) and the engine itself is currency-agnostic (operates on raw Doubles) — penny rounding assumes 2-decimal minor units, which breaks for zero-decimal currencies (JPY/KRW) where `*100/.../100` is wrong granularity.
- Caller-must-remember contract: ReceiptSplitCalculator caches the split (cachedSplitResult) and does NOT auto-invalidate on tip/total edits — callers must manually call updateSplits() or balances go stale (pinned, but fragile — TIP_EDIT_INVALIDATES_CACHE, ReceiptSplitCalculator.swift:172-195).
- Remainder/penny allocation always dumps the leftover cent on sortedIds.first (lexicographically smallest stableId) — deterministic but arbitrary/unfair; one participant systematically eats rounding remainder every receipt (ReceiptSplitEngine.swift:79-81).

- CURRENCY-BLIND THRESHOLD (the big one for a V2): matchThreshold=0.01 / warnThreshold=0.05 are absolute Doubles with no minor-unit scaling (Reconciler.swift:34-35). For JPY/KRW (zero-decimal) a 0.01 tolerance is effectively zero -> any rounding emits a finding; conceptually the bands should scale to the currency's minor unit. The delta DISPLAY is currency-aware (formatCurrency, Double.swift:49) but the COMPARISON is not -- a latent mismatch.
- EXTRAS DROPPED ON THE PERSISTENCE ROUND-TRIP: ScannedReceipt.toOCRSnapshot (OCRSnapshotBridge.swift:105-127) sums only .tax/.tip into typed slots and DROPS every other kind (.fee/.serviceCharge/.mandate/.surcharge/.discount/.credit/.unknown) -- the doc-comment at :92-98 admits it. Combined with OCRSnapshotMapper having only subtotal/tax/tip/total typed slots (OCRSnapshotMapper.swift:48-51), the LIVE V4 path can never carry a service charge / mandate / discount as its own categorized extra. Those amounts vanish from the snapshot, then reappear only if ReceiptItemsFixer re-scans raw OCR lines and re-adds them as .custom (-> .unknown) SummaryItems.
- DEAD CONTRACT BRANCHES: 6 of 9 ScannedExtraKind cases (.fee/.serviceCharge/.mandate/.surcharge/.discount/.credit) are never constructed in production -- only .tax/.tip/.unknown ever exist (V3ReceiptReconciler.swift:93-105 + OCRSnapshotBridge.swift:38-54). The elaborate positive/negative bucketing in totalFinding is exercised only by unit tests; real receipts route every non-tax/tip charge through .custom -> .unknown, which POISONS the total check (Reconciler.swift:69 returns nil). So in practice, any receipt with a service charge or discount silently loses its total-reconciliation entirely and only gets a 'Unknown extra' warn chip.
- HAND-MAINTAINED KIND ARRAYS with no exhaustiveness guard (Reconciler.swift:70-71): adding a new ScannedExtraKind requires remembering to add it to positiveKinds/negativeKinds or it's silently excluded from `expected`, producing a false .totalMismatch. A switch-with-no-default would have compiler-enforced this.
- TAX/TIP DOUBLE-SOURCE AMBIGUITY: ScannedReceipt.tax/.tip are COMPUTED by summing .tax/.tip extras (ScannedReceipt.swift:36-46), but OCRSnapshot stores tax/tip as flat typed Doubles. Round-tripping a receipt that had multiple tax lines through OCRSnapshot collapses them into one summed value, losing the multi-tax-line structure the contract claims to support (ScannedReceipt.swift:23-27 comment promises 'multi-tax lines').
- SEVERITY RECOMPUTED VIA SwiftData ROUND-TRIP, NOT FROM THE FRESH SNAPSHOT: updateReconciliationSeverity (ReceiptSnapshotApplying.swift:272) re-reads the just-written SwiftData rows via V3ReceiptReconciler.adapt rather than reconciling the in-memory ScannedReceipt directly, so any lossy write (e.g. ReceiptItemsFixer mutations, nil->1 quantity collapse) is baked into the verdict. The fresh ScannedReceipt that had richer extras is discarded before reconciliation.
- FINDINGS SHEET RECOMPUTES ON OPEN: ReconciliationFindingsSheet is fed V3ReceiptReconciler.report(for:).findings computed live at sheet-present time (ReceiptDetailView.swift:191), while the chip's severity is the persisted reconciliationSeverity string (ReceiptDetailView.swift:286). If the user edits items after scan, the chip (persisted) and the sheet (recomputed) can disagree -- only apply()/legacy paths call updateReconciliationSeverity, so manual edits don't refresh the persisted severity.
- missingExpectedField only fires on a TOTALLY empty scan (all three of subtotal/total/items nil/empty -- Reconciler.swift:87). A receipt with a total but no subtotal and no items is considered fine; there is no 'low overall OCR confidence' signal feeding reconciliation at all -- per-field confidence (OCRLineItem.confidence, ScannedExtra.confidence) is captured but NEVER consulted by the Reconciler.
- LIVE OCR CONFIG SHIPS EMPTY by design (OCRConfiguration.swift:36) -- production scans go through the Worker; the env/plist Azure-key branches are a dev escape hatch. - 9-case ScannedExtraKind taxonomy is mostly DEAD CODE today: fee/serviceCharge/mandate/surcharge/discount/credit have ZERO production producers — the Azure FieldsV4 DTO doesn't even decode those fields (ReceiptResultV4.swift:55-77). Adding UI/math for them would be building on a schema that never gets populated until the P3 'Reconciler' raw-text scanner ships (documented TODO at AzureDIv4Provider.swift:278-284).
- Lossy persistence boundary: even IF a non-tax/tip extra existed in-memory, toOCRSnapshot() drops it (OCRSnapshotBridge.swift:96-98). The persisted CloudKit shape (OCRSnapshot) has only scalar tax/tip — so a V2 'support service charges' feature requires changing the persisted schema, not just the in-memory type.
- Two divergent taxonomies (5-case SummaryItemType persisted vs 9-case ScannedExtraKind in-memory) with a non-isomorphic mapping: .custom<->.unknown collapses category identity (V3ReceiptReconciler.swift:100-101). A fee scanned as .surcharge cannot survive a persist->reload->re-adapt cycle as anything but .unknown/.custom.
- NO balance_due / amountDue field anywhere — receipt has no concept of a balance distinct from total. resolvedTotalAmount (Receipt.swift:264-280) is the only total notion. 'balance' in the codebase means person-level settle-up netBalance/hasOutstandingBalance (PersonDetailViewModel.swift, TripDetailPresentation.swift), not a receipt balance-due.
- NO inclusive-tax / VAT / GST flag. Only tax-related flag is calculationPreferences.calculateTipPostTax (Receipt.swift:4-9), which orders TIP relative to tax, not tax-inclusive pricing. International receipts with tax-included pricing cannot be modeled correctly.
- NO per-item tax: ReceiptItem (ReceiptItem.swift) and ScannedLineItem (ScannedReceipt.swift:75-94) have no tax field; tax is strictly a single receipt-wide SummaryItem. Cannot represent mixed tax rates (e.g. taxed alcohol + untaxed groceries).
- 'Shared item' EXISTS (ReceiptItem.sharedEqually / assignmentState .shared, ReceiptItem.swift:101-120) — but NO comp/void/discount-at-item concept. A comped or voided line can only be modeled by deleting it or zeroing customAmount; there is no isVoided/isComped flag, so it can't be shown as a struck-through $0 line.
- sharedByEveryone double-negative default (ReceiptItem.swift:101-103: !(sharedByEveryone == false)) means legacy/nil items silently default to shared-equally — fragile if a V2 change reads the raw private field instead of the sharedEqually accessor.
